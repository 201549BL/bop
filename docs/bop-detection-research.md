# Detecting bops reliably on AirPods

I recommend a single streaming detector that first asks whether the head performed a nod and then asks whether the nods form a bout. Use signed rotation to find the movement, attitude to check that the head moved away and came back, and autocorrelation to strengthen the rhythmic match. Keep the current autocorrelation build and the earlier stroke detector as baselines. Decide between candidates by replaying the same real AirPods sessions and comparing missed bops, false alerts per hour, and feedback delay.

## What should count

For the first evaluation, treat a bop as a short bout of deliberate rhythmic nods, including gentle nods and changes in size or speed. Give the bobblehead feedback as soon as it sees a credible nod in practice. In background listening, confirm the bout when a repeat provides enough evidence, then count it once. An isolated down-and-up movement could be a brief glance or a deliberate nod. Identical observed motion provides the same evidence in either case. Let the context determine how much evidence the app needs before it reacts. Apple’s head gestures are an example of a limited listening context: it asks users to nod or shake in response to an announced call or notification. [Apple AirPods user guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/airpods/devb2c431317/web)

Decide whether a rhythmic side sway, a horizontal shake, or a vertical bounce with the head held level should also count. Label those movements separately in the first recording set. The answer determines which of them are misses and which are false alerts. A narrow first evaluation of rhythmic nods is a proposed target, while the app currently supports a broader range of movements.

## What the evidence says

- **Natural movement is harder than a prompted gesture.** GrooveMeter is the closest published comparison. It gathered 926 minutes from 30 participants listening to music in several settings. Its head-motion F1 was 0.94 on a separate controlled set, 0.74 on the naturalistic set, and 0.72 in the office subset. Its naturalistic head-motion precision and recall were 0.72 and 0.75. It observed changing nod strength and direction within a bout, head motion during desk work, and repetitive signals from chewing and vehicle vibration. The motion sensor was a 70 Hz eSense earbud. The AirPod supplied the microphone, and the classifier used a seven-second window. Those results establish the difficulty of the task and the value of realistic negatives. They do not predict short-bout performance on Bop. [GrooveMeter, §§4.2, 5 and 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03295)
- **The available motion fields are enough for a prototype.** Apple documents streaming attitude, rotation rate and user acceleration on macOS and identifies the reporting earbud through sensor location. Core Motion also provides gravity. The AirPods can hand the stream from one bud to the other. A separate AirPods Pro and iPhone study observed motion samples on roughly 40 ms intervals and occasional missing samples. Treat this as one measured setup and measure the Mac stream directly. Bop’s 50 Hz grid interpolates between callbacks. It cannot restore a reversal missed in a gap. [Apple Core Motion at WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10179/) · [EarCardio, §3.2](https://xyzhang.ucsd.edu/papers/Ke.Sun_MobiCom26_EarCardio.pdf)
- **Shape matching and rhythm answer different questions.** A glasses-mounted IMU study used movement segmentation and dynamic time warping to classify six prompted head movements among quietly seated participants. This supports trying time-tolerant shape comparison, with a separate test for rejection during continuous use. In a different domain, the YIN paper shows how periodicity searches can prefer multiples of the true period. For Bop, use the time between plausible nods to constrain the lag, and check whether the strongest lag is a multiple of a shorter credible one. The studies do not establish suitable thresholds for Bop. [Li and Hu, §§2–4](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/10/5/123) · [YIN, §II](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/papers/deChevK02-yin.pdf)

## Baseline before Nod rhythm

The earlier rotation detector pairs opposing strokes with speed and angular excursion requirements. The experiment compares two adjacent windows of signed gyroscope data over periods from 0.30 to 1.40 seconds. It checks movement strength, sustained excursion, net rotation and normalized similarity. It can recover a repeated pattern that fails the stroke thresholds. In Auto it uses all three gyro axes without identifying whether the motion is primarily a nod or a head turn. Changing the correlation threshold alone cannot distinguish two traces that are identical apart from the axis in which they were performed. [Stroke detection](../Sources/BopCore/BopDetector.swift) · [Autocorrelation](../Sources/BopCore/RotationAutocorrelation.swift)

There is also a decision outside the rotation algorithm. In live use, a detection from **any** of rotation, orientation or vertical acceleration activates menu-bar feedback. The `Combined` result in Compare deduplicates and can associate evidence across channels during replay. It is a different path and also accepts strong single-channel events. Evaluate the actual live decision alongside its component detectors. Otherwise a rotation improvement can leave the most visible false triggers untouched. [Live session](../Sources/BopApp/BopSession.swift#L200) · [Compare](../Sources/BopCore/BopComparison.swift#L342)

The rotation count and bobblehead also use different evidence. Autocorrelation can count a gentle bout while the bobblehead’s rhythmic pulse is still waiting for a qualifying stroke. Time and validate the visible animation separately from bout confirmation. [Signal frame](../Sources/BopCore/BopComparison.swift#L130) · [Bobblehead feedback](../Sources/BopApp/BopSession.swift#L229)

## Candidate to test

1. **Find plausible nods.** Estimate gyro noise from quiet samples and freeze the estimate while movement is underway. Look for sustained signed motion followed by a reversal. Use the change in attitude from the start of the candidate to estimate excursion and how far the head returns. This gives a slow or gentle movement a way to qualify even if it has a low peak speed. Keep a modest absolute motion floor so a pattern in quiet sensor noise cannot qualify.
2. **Use direction as evidence.** Project gyro motion onto the plane perpendicular to gravity to emphasize pitch and roll, and retain the component parallel to gravity as evidence of turning. Estimate a consistent axis over the candidate and preserve its sign. Gravity identifies vertical, while a user’s head-forward direction still needs pose or calibration information if pure pitch must be distinguished from roll. Start with a soft penalty for turn-dominant motion if the target is nodding, and log the effect on natural, slightly diagonal bops.
3. **Confirm a run.** Compare successive plausible out-and-back movements in their own durations so one can be gentler or slower. Use timing and autocorrelation as supporting evidence. A strong repeated match can rescue a weak stroke if there is still credible angular excursion. A solid pair can qualify even if the windows have a poorer correlation score. Emit one event for the bout and rearm after the activity subsides. Reset evidence on a bud handoff or a meaningful sample gap, and do not count interpolated points as independent support.
4. **Use one live confirmation decision.** Rotation is the main evidence. Attitude can corroborate an excursion and return, though these are related measurements from the same device. Use vertical acceleration as supporting evidence or to identify an independently labeled bounce. Evaluate whether a bounce should activate the menu bar on its own. Send the same confirmed bout to the live menu and replay scorer while retaining per-signal diagnostics. For practice, try immediate visual feedback at a plausible turnaround with a separate full-bout confirmation.

Start with a few interpretable features and keep their scores in the replay output so each miss or false alert can be traced. If labeled data show the rules run out of room, compare a small classifier over those features or a limited time-warped shape comparison. The relevant comparison is whether the alternative catches more intended bouts at the same false-alert rate and acceptable delay.

## How to establish reliability

**First, get a paired recording set.** Use the current JSON export for the first ten or twenty labeled bouts, including deliberate weak movements, variable tempo, larger nods, and starts after a pause. Label bout start and end independently of Bop’s feedback, and mark the second turnaround when practical. Include negatives that look like the actual positive cases: alternating between the keyboard and screen, repeated conversational nods, turning between monitors, adjusting earbuds, shifting in the chair, chewing and walking. Include bops while typing or listening as a positive condition. A few clips are useful for debugging, while the current 20-second recording cap makes it impractical to estimate an hourly false-alert rate. Add a longer opt-in capture for uninterrupted desk use when building the benchmark. [Current recorder](../Sources/BopApp/BopSession.swift#L149) · [GrooveMeter motion confounds](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03295#S4.SS2.SSS1)

**Replay identical timestamped streams.** Compare the preceding stroke detector, the current autocorrelation build, and the candidate, with the decision policy for live feedback included. Tune on some sessions and hold out entire sessions and then people. Record bud source, pauses, sampling intervals and gaps. Test fresh earbud fits and source changes. A few people are enough for an initial pilot, but report how many people, bouts and listening hours each result represents. [GrooveMeter evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03295#S6.SS2) · [SHREC online gesture evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.06706)

**Score events.** Match detections to labeled bouts one to one with a predefined tolerance. Report bout recall overall and for gentle and variable-tempo bouts, false alerts per hour on uninterrupted background streams, excess alerts within a bout, and median and 90th-percentile delay from bout onset and the second turnaround. Compare sensitivity settings at the same false-alert rate. An illustrative pilot target is at least 90% bout recall, fewer than one false alert per hour, and confirmation around the second nod. These are proposed product targets. With zero events in three negative hours, a simple Poisson model still allows an upper 95% rate of about one event per hour, so report the exposure and spread it across sessions and people. [SHREC timing metrics](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.06706) · [Xu et al., continuous false-positive evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15239)

Public data are useful for supplemental tests. EarSet is open and offers ear-mounted gyro and acceleration for scripted nods, shakes, chewing, speaking, and walking on custom hardware. USI-HEAR includes nodding, shaking, speaking, eating and walking from 30 eSense users, with restricted files and a reported overwritten acceleration axis. IMUPoser contains AirPods motion but its published tensors expose orientation and acceleration without the original gyro stream. These releases cover subsets of the inputs and labels needed for the Bop comparison. [EarSet study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02762-3.pdf) · [EarSet data](https://zenodo.org/records/8142332) · [USI-HEAR record](https://zenodo.org/records/10843791) · [USI-HEAR dissertation, ch. 4](https://sonar.ch/documents/332436/files/2025INF009.pdf) · [IMUPoser dataset schema](https://github.com/FIGLAB/IMUPoser/blob/main/scripts/3.%20Dataset%20Walkthrough.ipynb)
